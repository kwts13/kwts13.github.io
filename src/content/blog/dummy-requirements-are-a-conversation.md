Early in my career I thought the job was to produce a requirements document. It took a few projects to work out that the document is just the receipt. The real work is the conversation that came before it.

## The document is the last step

By the time something is written down, the useful part is already over. Someone has been asked what they want, asked again what they meant, and asked a third time what happens if it goes wrong. That third question is where the actual requirements live.

## Questions I keep coming back to

- What are you trying to get done, as opposed to what are you asking me to build?
- Who else is affected by this, and have they been asked?
- What would make you change your mind?

That last one is my favourite. Everyone changes their mind eventually. It's much cheaper to know the conditions up front.

## Write it down anyway

None of this means skipping the document. People forget, teams change, and "I'm sure we agreed something different" is a very expensive sentence. Just don't mistake the artefact for the work.
