At some point at every party, someone asks what I do. I say I'm a business analyst, and usually end up over or under explaining what that means. They nod, wait a moment, and ask *what I do*. "It's complicated" is not an answer, so this is my attempt at a better one.

## The short version

I work out what a business needs before anyone builds anything, then check that what gets built is what we agreed on.

## The kitchen version

Say you're renovating your kitchen. You tell the builder you'd like an island bench. The builder can build an island bench.

A business analyst would ask why. Maybe you want somewhere to eat breakfast. Maybe you cook with other people and need room to move. Maybe you saw one on TV. Each answer leads to a different kitchen, and only one of them involves an island.

Then, three weeks in, you mention that the bench needs to seat six. Your partner would prefer a different colour. The builder has already ordered the stone.

Someone needs to keep track of what everyone wants, find where those wants conflict, write down the decisions, and make sure everyone is working from the same understanding of what type of kitchen you want. That's the job. Swap the kitchen for a piece of software, a data platform or a business process, and nothing else changes.

## What a normal week looks like

- **Talking to people.** A lot. Finding out what they do, what's annoying them and what they've been working around for so long they've finally asked you for help.
- **Writing things down.** Requirements, process maps, user stories. If it only exists in one person's head, it doesn't exist.
- **Translating.** The people who run the business and the people who build the systems use the same words to mean different things. Someone has to understand everyone.
- **Pointing out the awkward bit.** "You've asked for this, and you've asked for that, and they can't both be true."
- **Checking the result.** Does what got built do what we said it would?

## What it isn't

It isn't project management, which is about time, budget and people. It isn't development or testing either, though it overlaps with both. The titles blur on every team I've seen, which is fine as long as someone is clearly doing each of the jobs.

## The part I like

I like solving problems, and I like being the person people come to when they want something sorted. Most of the time the thing they asked for isn't quite the thing they need. Closing that gap is the best part of the work.

## The part I like less

Finding out, after the fact, that someone changed their mind. I've come to accept that this is also part of the job.

## So, did that answer it?

If you've read this far, you now know more about business analysis than most people at the party. If you'd rather talk about something else, I also play tabletop RPGs, which is a story for another post.
